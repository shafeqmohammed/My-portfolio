01 — Overview

Governed AI infrastructure, one experience

Sovereign AI on Azure Local lets organisations run AI workloads on infrastructure they control, within the boundaries their regulators and policies require. Administrators manage tenants, projects, people, infrastructure and governance — responsibilities that are often spread across separate tools.

The design work focused on making those responsibilities feel like one product: always clear which tenant and project you are in, who can do what, and whether what you are looking at complies with policy.

02 — The problem

Simple questions were hard to answer

Each concern — tenancy, identity, infrastructure, policy — comes with its own mental model. Administrators switch context constantly, and everyday questions become investigations:

?Which tenant and project am I acting in right now?
?Who can access this project — and why?
?Where does this data and compute live, and which policy applies?

04 — What made it difficult

Constraints

01Security and sovereignty are non-negotiable. Simplifying could never hide a permission or a policy boundary.
02Inherited access. Permissions flow from tenant to project to resource, which makes effective access hard to see.
03Platform dependencies. Underlying cloud and on-premises concepts had to be respected, not reinvented.
04Many stakeholders. Security, compliance, infrastructure and product teams each had legitimate and sometimes conflicting needs.
05 — Key decisions

Four decisions behind the experience

01

The project is the anchor

Every screen shows the current tenant and project. Resources, people, usage and policy are all reached from the project rather than from separate admin areas.

Trade-off: tenant-wide admins need a separate cross-project view for bulk tasks.

02

Describe roles as capabilities

Roles are explained by what people can do — “can deploy models”, “can view usage” — with inherited and direct access shown separately, so effective access is readable without decoding permission sets.

Trade-off: capability labels must stay mapped to the real permission model as it changes.

03

Governance in context

Residency and policy status appear on the projects and resources they govern, at the moment a decision is made, instead of in a separate compliance report.

Trade-off: more information on each object, so status uses one compact, consistent pattern.

04

Same patterns at every level

Tenant, project and resource administration share layouts, actions and terminology, so learning one level teaches the others.

Trade-off: some level-specific edge cases need progressive disclosure.
